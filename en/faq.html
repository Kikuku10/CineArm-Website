FAQ

The questions that come up most. Each answer is one paragraph; follow the link for detail.

Which focus motor is supported?

Air4 motors: wired supports one motor only (one cable, enter the controller address once); Bluetooth supports several, shown as Air4 A / Air4 B.

And not just focus: with custom output tracks a motor can drive anything that turns — zoom ring, mechanical iris, turntable, a rig you built.

Motors from other brands are not supported yet. See supported devices.

How far does Sony camera control go?

Over USB or WiFi you can set shutter, aperture, ISO, start and stop recording, drive focus position from the timeline, and pull live view into the app.

What is available depends on the body and firmware; on iPad the app controls the arm only, not the Sony camera. See supported devices.

Will my arm work?

Five Dobot models: Nova 5, CR5, CR10, CR10A (incl. CR10V), CR20A.

Anything else cannot connect yet, but you can still block shots on the virtual arm. See arm selection.

What are the cabinet, the pendant and host software?

In one line: the arm decides whether it can move, the cabinet how it moves, the pendant/official software who commands it, host software how the shot goes.

The cabinet runs motion and safety; the pendant is the handheld terminal; official software (DobotStudio Pro) is a software pendant; CineArm is host software that commands the arm through the cabinet to arrange shots.

Full explainer: how a robot arm actually moves.

What does a whole setup cost?

My own bill: used 10 kg-class arm 20k–30k CNY, steel base just over 1k, camera plate about 30, small parts a few hundred — around 26k in hardware.

A used 5 kg-class arm runs 10k–30k; new is roughly double. Turnkey packages have no list price. See price bands.

Can I practise without an arm?

Yes. Pick the virtual arm and rehearse positions, timeline, speed curves and follow focus with no hardware. Unlimited and free.

Is the free tier enough?

For practice and prep, yes — unlimited projects, virtual arms, previews and saves; real-arm connection and focus are open.

On a shoot the free tier allows 1 timeline and up to 4 pose keyframes — four pose keyframes are three move segments, enough for most shots; speed and focus tracks are unlimited. Studio (¥2,000 one-time) lifts that.

Do I have to pay before trying?

No. Download and use the free tier, pay only if it earns it — most kit software is buy-first, and I deliberately did the opposite.

Which platforms?

macOS (Apple Silicon, 14+), Windows 10/11 x64, iPad (self-signed). All three ship together; the app checks for updates itself.

How does the camera mount, and follow focus?

Via a flange plate at the tool tip, taking Arca and SmallRig quick releases. My CAD is open source — JLCPCB CNC 6061 sampling is about 30 CNY. Follow focus runs through the tool-tip port and is driven from the timeline.

How do I pick a used arm?

Six checks: vibration, its source, brake type, joint travel, collision sensitivity, payload parameters. Also confirm the hardware build — Leaderdrive vs reduced reducers, electromagnetic vs plunger brakes, 360° vs double 180° joints.

See inspection and hardware variants.

Will it crash? Is it safe?

Collaborative arms detect contact and stop compliantly, which is much safer for one-person shoots. Conditions: confirm compatibility, clear the area, physical E-stop in reach, first run slow and unloaded.

How do I get help, and does it cost?

Free: post a zip with the full reproduction steps and log capture in the WeChat group.

Paid: one-to-one support is ¥9,999 (includes Studio), for hands-on troubleshooting and remote sessions. I am one person, not a support team.

Who are you, and why build this?

A hobbyist — not a developer, not a company. My kit software could not do what I needed, the alternatives were five figures and sold per operating system, so I wrote my own and released it for people to try.
