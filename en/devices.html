Supported devices

Beyond the arm, two things decide what you can actually shoot: the follow-focus motor and the camera. Here is exactly how far support goes today.

Motors Sony cameras Other cameras How to verify

1. Motors: more than focus

The app has custom output tracks: bind a motor to anything that turns and it follows the timeline. Focus is just the most common use — zoom, mechanical iris, turntables and homemade rigs all work the same way.

Wired: one motor onlyThe motor plugs into the aviation connector on the tool flange and is powered and driven by the arm — the cabinet model is irrelevant.
Bluetooth: several motorsPair over Bluetooth and run multiple at once, shown as Air4 A, Air4 B and so on, each independent.
Bind it to a trackPick the motor, bind it to a track, and that track is now its responsibility — where it sits at each moment is set on the timeline.
Not supportedMotors from other brands do not connect yet.

What one motor can drive

Focus ringThe usual job: focus follows the subject without a hand on the ring.
Zoom ringPush and pull without a second operator; the zoom rides alongside the camera move.
TurntablePut the product on a rotating stage and have it spin evenly, hold, then reverse.
Mechanical iris, filter wheelIf it turns and the travel can be calibrated, it can be a track.
Homemade rigsSliders, lifts, flip mechanisms you built yourself — attach a motor and schedule it.
Two at onceOne pulls focus while the other zooms; both tracks run independently.
In one line

To the software, a motor is just a position-driven output. Focus is simply what it does most often — bind it to something else and it drives that instead.

Note

Every new mechanism needs a travel calibration: run it end to end, confirm both ends, and the software knows where 0% and 100% are. Without it the track shows as not executable — the hardware is fine.

2. Sony cameras: set parameters and drive focus from the timeline

Connect with a cable or WiFi and the app takes over.

ConnectingUSB or WiFi; the app lists online cameras and you pick one. Over WiFi the camera asks you to confirm the first time.
SettingsShutter, aperture and ISO are editable in the app. Which items appear depends on your body.
RecordingStart and stop from the app, with live recording state.
FocusYes — focus position can be driven from the timeline, scheduled alongside the arm's motion.
MonitoringThe camera image comes back into the app, so framing and focus sit on one screen.
RequiresA computer (macOS or Windows). On iPad the app controls the arm only, not the Sony camera.
Two honest caveats

① Not every Sony body exposes every feature: older models may record without exposing parameters, or without remote focus. The app shows what the body actually offers.

② Remote focus uses the lens's own AF, so the lens must support it; manual cinema lenses normally need the focus motor instead.

3. Other cameras

You can shoot without linking a camera at all — the arm only handles motion.

No camera linkPoses, speed and timeline as usual; focus comes from the motor, manual or scheduled.
Other brandsOnly Sony remote control is supported today; other brands are left alone.
MonitoringUse your own monitor or capture card; it does not affect the arm.

1. MotorConfirm it is an Air4; use Bluetooth for multiple motors, wired for a single, steadier link.
