My CR10A story

I paid 25,000 CNY for a used CR10A, spent over a month chasing problems, and returned it for a 23,799 CNY refund. This is not a complaint — the process itself is the most useful buying guide I have.

Timeline

01

10 October: bought

A used CR10A for 25,000 CNY from an industrial robotics company clearing idle assets. Described as fully functional.

02

It arrived with mismatched details

The arm label and the cabinet information did not agree; the firmware was old and the official software read an empty serial. The cabinet was a CC262 — the all-in-one type with the PSU inside.

03

No base, so I built one

Two steel plates at 113 kg each, plus other steel, close to 150 kg total. Two factories, both machined it wrong — that episode has its own section in the buying guide.

04

Mounted at last — and it really did shake

With the base stable, J3 showed a repeated impact whenever its velocity crossed zero, and a hop mid-move. The official pendant reproduced it too — which ruled out my software.

05

Building evidence

Video, microphone recordings of the joint noise (J3 on start, J2 too), and a written J3 reversal report with raw data — all with reproducible steps.

06

A month of negotiation, then a return

The seller first proposed a 20% penalty (5,000 CNY). After back and forth it ended as a return: a 23,799 CNY refund (25,000 − 1,000 depreciation − 201 freight), with me paying the return shipping.

Outcome: the arm went back and I shot nothing that month. A friend's used unit, bought for 18k, was steadier.

Four things this arm taught me

1. Test a used arm in personNot whether it moves, but slow arcs and velocity reversals. The fault only appeared in specific motion.
2. Separate vibration sources — but a hop is not vibrationBase stiffness, camera stabilisation and arm compliance all shake the image; an impact at zero velocity is usually hardware. Do not let “your base is too light” close the conversation.
3. Collect evidence from day oneVideo, audio, raw data, reproducible steps. “It shakes a lot” gets nowhere; curves and waveforms do.
4. Base and account matter more than the armNo base means no boot, the wrong cabinet means missing data, and an account can stay locked to the previous owner. Ask before paying.

Where things stand

Not the most flattering update, but it should be said.

The enthusiasm is goneAfter the return I am taking a break. A month of troubleshooting, negotiating and waiting was harder than writing the code.
No arm right nowNot a single one. Features can only be checked on the virtual arm, so hardware-related changes will be slow and may miss things.
Still file bugsUse GitHub Issues or the WeChat group. I will fix them, but I cannot promise dates.
Built for love, updated slowlyFree, closed source, one maintainer. Days are otherwise occupied, so releases are slow — better to say it now than leave you waiting.
所以

If you are waiting on a feature or a reply, it is probably not being ignored — it just has not come up yet. Use what works; if you cannot wait, do not wait for me. Blunt, but more honest than silence.
