Free vs Studio

The free tier is not a crippled demo. It limits exactly one thing: how much motion you can put in a single project. For most shots, that is enough.

Start here: four pose keyframes are three move segments

A pose keyframe records where the tip is and where it points at that instant. Between two adjacent pose keyframes there is one segment of motion — so four give you three segments.
Other tracks are not limited: speed curves, follow focus and so on can take as many keyframes as you like.

Is it enough?

A typical shot is “settle → push in → hold → pull out”, which is exactly three segments. Product shots, tabletop moves and orbit transitions mostly fit in three — the free tier's four pose keyframes cover the large majority of shooting.

When Studio actually matters

Unlimited pose keyframesComplex poses: multi-lap orbits, moving while zooming, layered push-ins. Once the segment count grows, four is not enough.
Unlimited timelinesKeep several candidate versions in one project, run them all and pick the one that feels right. That is a convenience for arranging, not a capability question.
Complex choreographyStacked moves, back-to-back direction changes, per-segment easing.
Rehearsal-heavy shootsMulti-position hand-offs and comparison-heavy setups.
