For newcomers

How a robot arm actually moves

Cinematic motion comes from a chain of parts working together: the arm, the control cabinet, the teach pendant and host software. Knowing who does what makes both buying and troubleshooting easier.

Five parts Control cabinet Pendant & official software Host software Connecting Shake suppression Glossary

1. Five parts make one working arm

Miss any of them and it does not move — which is also why used listings sell them separately.

01 · Arm
The arm itself
Six joints (J1–J6), each with a servo motor, a reducer (usually harmonic), a brake and an encoder. It sets payload, reach, speed and precision — and it sets the floor for vibration.
02 · Cabinet
Controller / control cabinet
The brain that actually runs the motion: servo drives, real-time planning, safety limits, the E-stop loop and IO. The arm is muscle; the cabinet is nerve.
03 · Pendant
Teach pendant (optional)
A handheld terminal with E-stop, enabling switch and joystick, used to jog the arm, record poses and run simple programs.
04 · Software
Official software / host software
The control end on a computer. Official software (e.g. DobotStudio Pro) is effectively a software teach pendant; CineArm is host software that arranges shots on a timeline. Both command the arm through the cabinet.
05 · Tool
End effector and adapters
Flange, camera plate, quick release, follow-focus motor — everything bolted to the tip lives here.
In one line

The arm decides whether it can move, the cabinet how it moves, the pendant/software who commands it, the tool what it shoots.

2. The control cabinet: PSU inside or outside

“Control cabinet” actually covers two shapes. This is the easiest item to overlook when buying used — and the cabinet type changes wiring, IO count and which options the software exposes.

All-in-one: PSU inside (e.g. CC262)

  • Controller and power supply share one enclosure
  • Bigger and heavier, with terminals and a fan
  • One piece to carry, but bulky and heat is concentrated
  • Common on CR-series setups

Split: PSU is a separate brick (e.g. CCBOX, SimBox)

  • A small controller box plus an external adapter
  • Small footprint, easy to travel with
  • One more cable — protect it on set
  • Common on Nova-series setups

Three things to know

1. One arm, different cabinetsThe same Nova 5 may ship with a SimBox or a DSC. The firmware reports the cabinet type (for example DT-SimBox-0); ask the seller for a photo of the controller's system page.
2. Some “cabinets” are just a shellSome third-party packages simply put the controller and PSU inside one box. Nothing improves — the size and cabling change, not the capability. Do not pay for it as an upgrade.
3. The cabinet decides three thingsIO count, whether functional safety exists (E-stop circuit class), and which options the software exposes. These affect how external buttons and a safety loop are wired.
Focus is not the cabinet's business

Wired follow focus plugs into the aviation connector on the tool flange and runs through the arm — the cabinet model does not matter, any cabinet works.

Bluetooth focus is not cabinet-limited either: pair wirelessly, use as many motors as you like.

WiFi is plugged in, not built in

Where it comes fromThe controller has no radio by itself. Add a USB WiFi adapter and it can join a router or broadcast its own hotspot.
Changes need a rebootAfter adjusting WiFi settings the controller has to restart — do not mistake it for a dead connection on set.
Not every adapter worksCheap adapters sometimes see no signal; a different model fixes it. Confirm the model, or just use Ethernet.
Keep the control link wiredWireless is for remote control only. Latency and packet loss make the arm itself judder — covered in the buying guide.
Buying used

Take the cabinet with the arm, and confirm they belong together. Without it the arm is a paperweight; swapping cabinets or upgrading firmware may also require reactivation or re-binding — ask before you pay.

3. Pendants, and the official software that is one

They are often conflated; they do the same job — commanding the arm by hand.

Teach pendant (hardware)

  • Handheld, with E-stop and enabling switch
  • Fastest way to jog and record poses on site
  • No computer needed
  • Great for touch-ups and recovery

Official software (e.g. DobotStudio Pro)

  • Runs on a computer; functionally a software pendant
  • Jog, record poses, simple programs, status and logs
  • Bigger UI, clearer parameters, better for debugging
  • Many parameters (damping, speed ceiling, collision sensitivity) live only here
Why another tool

Pendants and official software are good at “record this pose, then go to that pose”. Filming needs rhythm — when to accelerate, when to ease out, keeping the lens on the subject, driving follow focus. That is the gap host software fills.

4. Host software: where CineArm sits

It replaces neither the cabinet nor the pendant — it sits on top and decides “how the shot goes”.

Top
Host software (CineArm)
Block poses, drag a timeline, shape speed curves, lock the aim, drive follow focus — then send the whole motion to the cabinet.
↑ sends one trajectory
Middle
Control cabinet
Interpolates in real time, checks limits and safety, drives the six joints.
↑ outputs joint commands
Bottom
Arm and tool
Motors, reducers and brakes execute; the camera rides the arc.

Pendants and official software attach at the cabinet level too — all three can coexist and switch freely.

What CineArm does

  • Compose shots on a timeline
  • Speed curves and easing
  • Target lock and follow-focus linkage
  • Rehearse on a virtual arm

What it does not do

  • Replace the cabinet's real-time control or safety
  • Change hardware parameters (still in official software)
  • Take over the E-stop circuit

5. Connecting

One rule: keep the control link wired.

Ethernet (recommended)Lowest latency and most stable; the default for long shoots.
USBSupported on some models; simpler cabling, less robust over long runs or in noisy environments.
WirelessRemote control only — latency and packet loss make the arm itself judder.
After connectingThe software reads back model, firmware and limits. Wrong readouts point at protocol revision or the cabinet.

6. Let us define “shake suppression”

The term gets used very loosely. Trajectory smoothing and speed curves are ordinary motion planning, not shake suppression. Real suppression lives inside the control cabinet, not on the computer.

A comparison like the one above — the same joint moving 30° in 4 s, two smoothing methods — is motion planning. It makes the move smoother, it does not remove vibration.

Two different things both called “smoothing”

Path smoothing (CP smoothing)

  • If three points form a corner, smoothing makes the move round the second point instead of stopping there and turning
  • The cost: the path no longer passes exactly through that point
  • The amount is adjustable — more smoothing, more deviation
  • If that corner was “skim past the edge of the object”, it gets shaved off

Speed-curve smoothing (S-curve, quintic)

  • The path shape is unchanged — still the same straight segments
  • What changes is speed over time: softer start, gentler stop
  • Fixes “jerky start, abrupt stop”, not “corners rounded off”
  • Adjusts acceleration and jerk, not position
In one line

Path smoothing changes where it goes; speed-curve smoothing changes how fast it goes. Both are motion planning, neither is shake suppression — suppression changes how the servo follows.

One hard conclusion

Any setup where the computer plans the trajectory and the cabinet executes it has no shake suppression. Host software decides where and how fast; whether it vibrates on the way is settled by the cabinet's servo loop.

In other words: suppression belongs to the cabinet, and the cabinet belongs to the arm maker. Unless a vendor can genuinely modify the cabinet firmware with the manufacturer, “our shake-suppression algorithm” usually means a smoother curve.

How to test it in the room

Asking a turnkey vendor “do you have shake suppression?” proves nothing. Ask for a comparison:

Same move, two sendersSend it once from host software, then once from the official pendant — same arm, same speed, same path.
CompareIdentical shaking on both means there is no suppression at all — what they call suppression is just smoothing. If only the pendant is steady, suppression exists only inside the vendor's own chain. If both are steady, suppression sits in the cabinet and applies to any source — that is the real thing.
Sharper questionAsk whether suppression can be switched off and re-run. If it can be disabled and gets visibly worse, the feature is real and configurable.
Do not get lost in terms“Smoothing”, “vibration control”, “anti-shake” get mixed together. Ask: does this change the path, or the servo? Changing the path is planning.

7. Words people ask about

PayloadMass the tip can carry (camera plus plate), in kg.
Reach / radiusFurthest the tip can go — how far the shot can stretch.
J1–J6Joint numbers: J1 rotates the base, J6 rotates the tip.
TCPTool centre point; all coordinates are relative to it.
BrakeLocks joints when power drops; electromagnetic holds stiffer than plunger.
ReducerGearbox in each joint, usually harmonic; its compliance is one source of vibration.
Protocol revisionComms rules between software and cabinet; V3 and V4 differ in features.
RepeatabilityDeviation when returning to the same point, in mm.
